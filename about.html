About GATOKA

An emerging African initiative bringing medical and engineering knowledge together to support a stronger future for medical technology.

our BACKGROUND

An initiative shaped by medicine, engineering, and a shared ambition.

GATOKA is being developed by healthcare professionals and clinical engineers who recognise the critical necessity of building sovereign, decentralized technical capabilities within African health ecosystems.

Grounded in clinical reality and honest about our early pre-operational stage, the initiative focuses on the fundamental prerequisite of medical technology: understanding diagnostic equipment at the circuit and firmware level before advancing to maintenance and regional assembly.

SYSTEMIC CONTEXT

Why GATOKA began.

Across public hospitals and regional clinics, diagnostic continuity is systematically disrupted by external dependencies. High-value ultrasound scanners, telemetry consoles, and neonatal support units frequently sit idle after minor subsystem errors because proprietary firmware locks, lack of local calibration schematics, and absent regional supply chains prevent on-site intervention.

GATOKA began with a commitment to address this underlying reality. Instead of relying indefinitely on short-term equipment donations or costly international fly-in technician arrangements, the initiative is structured around cultivating domestic engineering sovereignty — training certified clinical engineering cadres capable of diagnosing, servicing, and sustaining equipment across its full lifecycle.

COLLABORATIVE METHODOLOGY

Progress begins with shared knowledge.

Sustainable medical technology cannot be engineered in isolation from the clinical environments in which it operates. GATOKA is premised on close, systematic collaboration between bedside practitioners who understand clinical realities and engineers who master electrical design, signal processing, and mechanical reliability.

By structuring early dialogue across medical wards, polytechnic institutions, and independent technical workshops, we intend to establish a continuous feedback loop: identifying diagnostic friction directly from hospital wards, translating it into bench-level engineering solutions, and distributing technical knowledge openly.
